- [verified-official] The Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) is Act No. 13 of 2081. It received assent and was published on 2081.12.03 (16 March 2025) and came into force on the 31st day after assent (s1(2)). It also applies to persons outside Nepal who provide goods or services within Nepal through e-commerce (s1(3)).
  - Sources: विद्युतिय व्यापार (इ-कमर्स) ऐन, २०८१ – Nepal Law Commission <https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/13517/electricity-trade--e-workers--act--2081/>; Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – Nepal Law Commission English translation (PDF) <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: The Act is in force and binds DripNepal. Treat its requirements as launch blockers in the PRD. Cite the Nepali text as authoritative, because the English version is a translation.
  - Fact-check: supported — Page 1 of the Law Commission English PDF reads 'Act no. 13 of the Year 2081 (2025)' and gives assent and publication as 2081.12.03 (March 16, 2025). s1(2) says 'come into force on the 31st day of assent', and s1(3) covers persons outside Nepal. The Law Commission page (content/13517) has an odd URL slug but is titled 'विद्युतिय व्यापार (इ-कमर्स) ऐन, २०८१' and links this PDF. The English text is a translation; the Nepali text governs.
- [verified-official] The Act defines an 'Intermediary Business Entity' as one that facilitates the sale of a seller's goods or services to a purchaser through an electronic platform. A 'List-Based' entity sells its own listed goods directly. An 'Electronic Platform' includes websites, apps, software, internet, intranet and social-media marketplaces (s2(g),(k),(o)).
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: As a multi-vendor marketplace, DripNepal is most likely an intermediary business entity (s14 duties). If it ever sells its own stock, s15 list-based duties also apply. Model a seller_type or fulfilment-owner field per listing.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s2(g), (k) and (o). Also relevant: s2(f) says 'Seller' includes a list-based entity, and the s2(l) proviso says a platform used only to inform about or promote goods is not e-commerce.
- [verified-official] Section 4(2) requires the platform to show, at least: platform name; business name, address, registering authority and registration number; registered and head office and branches; any special licence; whether it is intermediary or list-based; VAT or PAN number; contact details (email, phone, mobile, social links, customer-service contacts); the grievance officer or unit's contact details and address; and the e-commerce portal listing number. Changes must be updated within 48 hours (s4(3)).
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: Build an admin-editable 'Business information' page and footer block with every s4(2) field, including the DoCSCP listing number and grievance officer. Record the time of each change in an audit log to show the 48-hour update.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s4(2)(a)-(i). (g) also lists telefax. s4(3) sets 48 hours. s4(4) requires the details to be complete, clear and easily accessible. The s4(1) proviso lets micro or small entrepreneurs sell through other platforms.
- [verified-official] Under s5, after setting up its platform a business must apply electronically to be listed on the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection (DoCSCP) e-commerce portal. The application gives business, owner or director identity, VAT/PAN and contact details. The Department issues a listing number within 7 days, and branch or outlet changes must be updated within 7 days.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: Getting DoCSCP listing is a pre-launch operations task. Store the listing number as configuration and show it on the site. Operating without listing is an offence under s21(b).
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s5(1)(a)-(g), s5(3) (7 days from receipt of the application) and s5(4) (branch or outlet changes within 7 days). The claim omits s5(2): businesses already trading when the Act started had 3 months to apply.
- [verified-official] Under E-Commerce Directive 2082 s4(3), the listing application must also include: the firm registration certificate and proof it is current; proof of e-invoice permission (for those issuing electronic bills); proof of domain ownership (own platform) or a hosting agreement (other platform); a privacy protection policy and a return policy; and a system test report against any GoN cyber-security standard, if one has been issued.
  - Sources: विद्युतीय व्यापार (इ-कमर्स) निर्देशिका, २०८२ – Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies <https://moics.gov.np/content/13547/electronic-business--e-commerce--directory--2082/>; E-Commerce Directive 2082 (scanned signed PDF) <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>
  - Implication: A published privacy policy and a published return policy are required for listing. Plan for a security test report, and keep the domain registration in the company's name.
  - Fact-check: supported — Checked against the scanned page images (page 2). The PDF's OCR text layer is unreadable. s4(3)(क)-(ङ) match the claim: registration certificate and proof it is current; e-invoice permission proof; domain ownership or hosting agreement; privacy protection policy and return policy; a system test report if GoN has issued a cyber-security standard.
- [verified-official] The Directive requires the Department to issue the listing within 7 days of a complete application (s3(2)). Changes to name, address, ownership, share structure or nature of business, or a new outlet, must be updated within 7 days (s3(3)). If an unlisted firm is found, an inspection officer orders listing within 7 days, after which the Department proceeds to cancel the platform (s5). Listing is free (s7(2)). The Directive takes effect from the date of Ministry approval (s1(2)). The MoICS page is dated 17 Falgun 2082.
  - Sources: E-Commerce Directive 2082 (scanned signed PDF) <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>; E-Commerce Directive 2082 – MoICS page <https://moics.gov.np/content/13547/electronic-business--e-commerce--directory--2082/>
  - Implication: Add an ops runbook item to re-file listing details within 7 days of any corporate change, such as a new warehouse or fulfilment centre (defined as an 'outlet').
  - Fact-check: supported — Checked on the page images. s3(2): 7 days, and if details are incomplete, 7 days from when the complete details are submitted. The s3(3) explanation lists name, address, ownership, share structure and nature of business. s5: the officer also takes action under the Act (which can include a fine) and orders listing within 7 days; if the firm still does not list, the Department starts cancelling (खारेज) the platform. s7(2): listing, updates and cancellation are free. The MoICS page shows '१७ फागुन, २०८२'.

It says businesses must set up a platform (s4) and list on the Department portal (s5), and warns of strict legal action against unlisted or illegal platforms. The homepage links https://online.doc.gov.np/onlineuser. Better source: https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%20%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%82%E0%A4%9A%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%A4%20%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%80%20%E0%A4%9C%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%80%20%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%82%E0%A4%9A%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%BE-e-commerce_mh1zzbj.pdf
- [verified-official] Section 6 requires each product listing to disclose: name, nature, design, trademark, image and weight or substance; the final selling price including tax; any extra delivery charges; usage precautions; delivery date and time; payment methods; warranty or guarantee duration and conditions; the producer; country of origin for imports; post-purchase conditions; whether the item is returnable and, if so, the conditions and duration; whether an order can be cancelled before dispatch; manufacture and expiry dates; key contract terms in language the general public understands; and the review, rating, grievance and feedback mechanism.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: The product data model needs required fields for brand or trademark, producer, country of origin, a returnable flag with a return window and conditions, cancellation policy, warranty, care or precaution text, and estimated delivery. Always display VAT-inclusive final prices plus delivery charges before checkout. Failing to disclose is an offence (s21(c)).
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s6(a)-(p). It omits s6(l) (place and conditions for services), which is not relevant to goods. For an intermediary, sellers must supply this data (s16(c)) and the platform must display it accurately (s14(a)).
- [verified-official] Electronic transactions are deemed valid contracts, and the contract must set out terms on delivery, cancellation, return, exchange, warranty, guarantee and refund (s7). Payment may be made before, during or after delivery, and payment to the delivery service provider is deemed payment to the business, which covers cash on delivery (s8(1)). After payment, an electronic or physical invoice and a payment receipt must be given immediately and a record kept (s8(3)).
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: Order confirmation must link to the terms that applied at checkout. Keep a version of the terms per order. Issue the invoice and receipt automatically at payment, and for COD at courier collection. Model a collected_by_courier state that counts as paid.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s7(1)-(2) and s8(1),(3). The Act says 'prior to, during, or after the purchase or transfer'. The Act never says 'cash on delivery'; that is an inference, though a reasonable one. Tension to note: Directive s9(1) says only NRB-approved digital payment gateways may be used, yet s9(2) contemplates cash payment (an e-invoice is mandatory).
- [verified-official] Goods must be delivered to the designated location on the date or time, or within the period, stated in the contract (s9(1)). Buyers may collect from a sales centre (s9(2)). Place or time can be changed before dispatch by mutual agreement, with charges adjusted (s9(4)-(5)). Late delivery makes the business and seller liable to accept cancellation or return and refund (s15(h), s16(g)). No fixed statutory number of delivery days was found in the Act or the Directive.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>; E-Commerce Directive 2082 <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>
  - Implication: Each order must carry a promised delivery date or window, which acts as a contractual SLA. Support a pre-dispatch address or time change flow and a buyer cancellation or refund flow when delivery is late. Pickup points are optional but supported by law.
  - Fact-check: weakly-supported — s9(1),(2),(4),(5) are correct. I read the whole Act and all 5 Directive pages and found no fixed number of delivery days. However, s15 applies only to list-based entities. For an intermediary like DripNepal the relevant duties are s14(f) (accept return, exchange or refund under the law, whatever the contract says), s16(d) (seller delivers on time, force majeure excepted) and s16(g) (seller accepts return or cancellation for late delivery and refunds). The citation to s15(h) is wrong for a marketplace.
- [verified-official] Under s10, if goods do not conform to the s6 description, the buyer may return them unused and undamaged, and the business must accept them unconditionally. It must offer an exchange on request, or otherwise refund the amount paid including taxes. Directive 2082 s9(3) requires such refunds within 7 days.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>; E-Commerce Directive 2082 <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>
  - Implication: The returns module needs a 'not as described' reason, exchange-or-refund choice, a refund including VAT, and a 7-day refund SLA timer with alerts. Refunds need reverse entries or credit notes on the invoice side.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s10(1)-(4). Directive s9(3) reads: refund under Act s10(4) 'must be made within 7 (seven) days'. s10(5) leaves other return rules to federal law, i.e. the CPA s14 7-day return (see the CPA claim).
- [verified-official] Section 14 obliges an intermediary to: show accurate s6 details; keep transaction records for the period set by tax law; ensure warranties are honoured; not discriminate among sellers of the same category, and clearly show buyers any special preference given to a seller; enter an agreement with each seller before listing; and accept return, exchange or refund under the law regardless of contract terms. Section 20 says it cannot refuse to handle complaints on the ground that it did not make, import or directly supply the goods.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: Vendor onboarding needs an e-signed seller agreement before a vendor's first listing goes live. Search and ranking must be seller-neutral, and paid or boosted placements must be visibly labelled (for example 'Sponsored'). The marketplace owns buyer complaints and cannot redirect them solely to the vendor.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s14(a)-(f), including the (d) proviso on disclosing preferential treatment. s20 applies to complaints under s28 and grievances under s33.
- [verified-official] Under s16, sellers must: sign a written or electronic contract with the intermediary before selling; provide evidence of business registration, documents, full name and address, grievance mechanism, return and refund details, and PAN or VAT details; supply the s6 information for each product; deliver on time; avoid unfair trade practices; not post fake reviews; and accept returns, cancellations and refunds for goods that are non-conforming, late or defective.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: The vendor KYC flow must collect the registration certificate, PAN or VAT number and certificate, legal name and address, and return policy. Store them with document uploads and a verification status. Block selling until KYC is verified.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s16(a)-(g). s16(d) excuses force majeure.
- [verified-official] The Act prohibits posting or causing reviews, ratings or feedback while impersonating a fictitious consumer (s15(c), s16(f)), and misleading advertising (s15(d)). Directive 2082 s10(1) bans misleading or exaggerated information and advertisements on the platform.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>; E-Commerce Directive 2082 <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>
  - Implication: Allow reviews only from verified purchases. Block vendors from reviewing their own products. Add moderation and audit trails, and a content review step for vendor product claims.
  - Fact-check: supported — The text is accurate, but s15(c)-(d) bind list-based entities and s16(f) binds sellers. s14 puts no explicit fake-review duty on intermediaries, though s19 (unfair trade) applies to all parties. Directive s10(1) confirmed: 'भ्रामक वा अतिरञ्जित सूचना तथा विज्ञापन प्रकाशन गर्न पाइने छैन'.
- [verified-official] Section 12 requires a business to keep users' personal information confidential and not disclose or use it except as the law permits. Exchanging transaction information among the buyer, the business and the delivery provider is allowed. The business must not deprive users of the ability to access the platform, enter or modify their personal information, or deactivate identity-related sources (s12(3)).
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: Provide self-service profile editing and account deactivation or closure. Limit sharing of buyer data with vendors and couriers to what fulfilment needs, such as name, phone and address.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s12(1)-(3). s12(4): a breach of s12(2) is punished under other prevailing law.
- [verified-official] Under s33, a complaint must be registered and acknowledged immediately, investigated and decided within 15 days, and answered in writing within 15 days, with reasons if it cannot be resolved. The business must run an online redressal mechanism. Directive s12 adds that unresolved complaints can go to the Department, which must resolve them within 15 days. Directive s13 creates an e-commerce dispute resolution committee that works by mediation.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>; E-Commerce Directive 2082 <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>
  - Implication: Build a ticketing module with an immediate acknowledgement (SMS or email plus a ticket ID), a 15-day SLA clock, required written resolution text, escalation info pointing to DoCSCP, and retention of ticket history.
  - Fact-check: supported — Matches s33(1)-(6). Directive s12(1) requires online complaint registration under Act ss28 and 33. s12(2)-(3): a complaint can go to the Department if not resolved under Act s33(5), and must be resolved within 15 days of receipt. s13: the committee resolves disputes through 'छलफल तथा मध्यस्थता' (discussion and mediation).
- [verified-official] Section 22 sets a fine of Rs 20,000 to Rs 100,000, imposed by an inspection officer, for: operating without a platform or listing; failing to disclose s6 details; or breaching s14(a),(b),(e), s15(a),(b),(e),(f) and s16(a)-(d). Section 23 sets a fine of Rs 50,000 to Rs 500,000, or 6 months to 3 years' imprisonment, or both, for breaching s14(c),(d),(f), s15(c),(d),(g),(h),(i) and s16(e),(f),(g).
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>
  - Implication: Seller neutrality (s14(d)), warranty compliance and returns (s14(f)) carry criminal exposure. Rank them as high-severity requirements in the threat model and risk register.
  - Fact-check: supported — The mapping to s21(a)-(d) and s21(e) is correct. s22(1) sets 20,000 to 1 lakh (appeal to the Director General within 7 days, s22(2)). s23(1) sets 50,000 to 5 lakh, or 6 months to 3 years, or both. s23 cases are state cases filed in the consumer or district court (s24-25).
- [verified-official] Section 31 applies the consumer protection law, mutatis mutandis, to quality, labelling, procedures and grounds for returning goods, pricing, inspection, dispute resolution, compensation and consumer rights for goods or services traded electronically, unless the E-Commerce Act says otherwise.
  - Sources: Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025) – English translation <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/E-Commerce%20Act%2C%202081_yr7k9o5.pdf>

- [verified-official] Directive 2082 s8(1) requires platforms to store users' passwords, phone or mobile numbers, addresses, dates of birth and other sensitive personal details used for authentication securely in encrypted form. Other general details must be protected against unauthorised access, misuse or leakage with appropriate technical and administrative measures.
  - Sources: E-Commerce Directive 2082 <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>
  - Implication: Hash passwords (for example with argon2 or scrypt). Use column-level or application-level encryption for phone, address and date of birth, not only encryption at rest, and a blind-index hash for lookups such as phone login. Record this in the threat model and data model.

- [verified-official] Directive 2082 s8(2) says that if there is unauthorised access, leakage of user information or a platform fault, the business must immediately stop transactions and resume only after recovery. It must also inform the public about the incident.
  - Sources: E-Commerce Directive 2082 <https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/ecommerce-directives_8errkt4.pdf>

- [verified-official] Directive 2082 s14 requires e-commerce platforms to keep records of transactions, bills and invoices, consumer complaints and their hearing, and Department inspection and supervision details for at least five years.
